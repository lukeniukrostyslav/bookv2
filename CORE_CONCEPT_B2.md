# B2 — CORE CONCEPT

Date: 2026-09-20
Status: COMPLETE

## Design target
A standalone commercial psychological mystery/thriller with a high-concept premise, emotional stakes, a distinctive atmosphere, fair-play clues, and a reveal architecture that rewards rereading.

## Candidate concepts

### C1 — THE LAST RECORDING
A sound archivist receives an audio file recorded by a woman exactly twelve hours after the official time of her death. The recording contains ordinary details from the next morning — including a private sentence only the archivist could know. When he investigates, he discovers that the recording is one of several impossible records connected to people who vanished from the same coastal town.

Strengths: audio as a mystery device; intimate tension; strong atmosphere; multiple clue channels.
Risk: supernatural interpretation can become familiar unless the underlying mechanism is original.

### C2 — THE HOUSE THAT REMEMBERS
A restorer is hired to catalogue the contents of an abandoned villa before demolition. Every room contains objects arranged as if someone has reconstructed a specific crime scene. The final room contains a photograph of the restorer standing inside the house — taken decades before he was born.

Strengths: visual atmosphere; contained setting; personal connection; strong opening image.
Risk: time/identity mechanics need rigorous rules to avoid feeling arbitrary.

### C3 — THE NAME IN THE LEDGER
A municipal archivist discovers that every major disappearance in a city over forty years has one impossible common detail: a dead person's name appears in the official ledger exactly one day before the disappearance. Then the archivist finds his own name — dated tomorrow.

Strengths: immediate hook; procedural investigation; escalating deadline; easy reader-facing premise.
Risk: "predicted death" territory is familiar; requires a highly distinctive explanation and emotional core.

### C4 — THE SILENT FLOOR
In an old apartment building, residents insist that there are eleven floors. The architectural plans show twelve. A fire inspector discovers evidence of a sealed twelfth floor and a list of residents who officially never lived there. One name belongs to the inspector's missing sister.

Strengths: claustrophobic atmosphere; mystery naturally unfolds through physical exploration; personal stakes.
Risk: hidden-room premises are common; needs an exceptional mythology and character layer.

### C5 — THE SECOND FUNERAL
A man attends his own funeral because an unknown person has sent him an invitation bearing his full identity. Everyone at the funeral recognizes him, but nobody admits knowing him. During the ceremony, a stranger whispers: "Do not let them see that you remember."

Strengths: immediate cinematic hook; psychological tension; identity mystery.
Risk: identity/memory twist space is crowded; requires disciplined originality and payoff.

## Selection

### Chosen working concept: C1 — THE LAST RECORDING

Reason for selection:
- The audio premise creates a concrete, unusual mystery without requiring supernatural assumptions.
- It supports intimate psychological tension and an investigative engine.
- Sound can become a recurring structural device: what is heard, what is missing, background noises, edits, silence and timing.
- It gives the protagonist a profession directly connected to the mystery.
- It can sustain a full novel without relying on a single gimmick.
- It leaves room for a grounded explanation while preserving ambiguity during the investigation.

## Core premise — canon

A sound archivist receives a recording that appears to have been made twelve hours after the recorded speaker's official death. The recording contains a private sentence that should be impossible for the speaker to know. The archivist begins investigating and discovers a pattern connecting other impossible recordings to disappearances from the same coastal town.

## What is NOT canon yet

- protagonist's final name
- exact town
- exact year
- identity of the recorded woman
- cause/mechanism behind the recordings
- antagonist
- final reveal
- ending

These remain OPEN until later blocks.

## Reader promise

A tense investigation built around the question:
**How can a dead person leave a truthful recording after death — and why was it sent to him?**

The novel must eventually provide a coherent answer rather than relying on an unexplained twist.
