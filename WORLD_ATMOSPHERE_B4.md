# B4 — WORLD & ATMOSPHERE

## Canonical setting

### Porto Nero
A fictional Ligurian coastal town in northern Italy. Population: approximately 18,000 permanent residents, larger in summer.

Porto Nero is built around a narrow natural harbour. The old town climbs the western slope above the water; newer apartment blocks and hotels occupy the eastern side. A disused freight rail line runs behind the waterfront and ends near an abandoned customs warehouse.

The town's central tension is between the postcard surface presented to tourists and the older, half-forgotten industrial town underneath it.

### Weather and sensory identity
- frequent winter rain and sea fog;
- salt on windows and metal railings;
- gulls, ferry horns and fishing engines;
- wet stone streets;
- old shutters and narrow stairways;
- the smell of diesel, coffee and seaweed near the harbour;
- sudden silence when fog closes the waterfront.

Sound is a major storytelling element. Background noise must sometimes carry clues.

## Key locations

### Municipal Archive
A former customs office converted into a municipal archive. Thick walls, high windows, basement storage and a restoration room built around old recording equipment.

### Via della Marina, 6
The former Venturi residence. Demolished in 2019. Official records state that Mara Venturi died there.

### The Old Customs Warehouse
A locked waterfront structure near the end of the disused freight line. Its history is tied to the town's older shipping trade.

### Hotel Aurora
Built on the site of the demolished Venturi building. Modern exterior; underground service corridors preserve parts of the older foundation.

### Harbour
Fishing boats leave before dawn. Tourist boats arrive in summer. The harbour's changing soundscape can establish time and location.

## Historical layer

Porto Nero expanded around fishing, small-scale shipping and customs work. During the late twentieth century the freight trade declined. Several industrial buildings were abandoned or converted.

The town has a culture of remembering selectively: official commemorations preserve respectable history while uncomfortable incidents survive mostly through private stories.

## Social atmosphere

Permanent residents know one another's families. People are polite to outsiders but protective of old stories. The archive is trusted because it is supposed to preserve facts, which makes corrupted or contradictory records particularly disturbing.

## Storytelling rules

1. Never use the setting as decorative filler.
2. Every major location should have a sensory signature.
3. Sound can identify distance, time, movement or hidden activity.
4. Tourism and local memory should create social pressure.
5. The town's history may contain clues, but no historical fact becomes a supernatural explanation automatically.
6. New world facts must be recorded in STORY_BIBLE and CONTINUITY_LEDGER.
