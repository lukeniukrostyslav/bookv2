# B11 LINE EDIT — CHAPTERS 9–16

## Chapter 9 — Three Voices

Elia did not begin with the disappearances.

He began with the sound.

He copied Mara's recording three times and kept the original untouched.

Then he compared the background pulse with two older archive recordings.

The first belonged to Carlo Bassi, a fisherman missing since 2008.

The second belonged to Paolo Ferri, a customs electrician missing since 2011.

The files were unrelated in the catalogue.

But the pulse was identical.

Thirty-one seconds.

Elia marked each occurrence.

Lucia stood beside him.

"Could be the same equipment."

"That's the working hypothesis."

"Not the conclusion."

"I know."

She smiled faintly.

"You learn quickly."

Elia isolated the pulse.

A relay.

A motor.

Then a tiny click.

Three recordings.

Three dates.

One mechanical signature.

He opened the case files.

Both men had last been seen near the waterfront.

That was the first fact that mattered.

Not the pulse.

The location.

## Chapter 10 — Thirty-One Seconds

Elia spent four hours trying to disprove his own theory.

He compared natural echoes.

Wind.

Harbour engines.

Electrical hum.

None matched the interval.

The pulse returned every thirty-one seconds with mechanical consistency.

Lucia watched from the doorway.

"How certain?"

"About the interval? Certain."

"About what causes it?"

"Not yet."

He played the sound through headphones.

At the edge of the signal was a relay switching.

Then a motor.

Old equipment.

He wrote:

**31-second mechanical cycle.**

He did not write "network."

Not yet.

He had learned something from Lucia.

A clue was not an explanation.

## Chapter 11 — The Old System

Tomas was reluctant to talk.

"I know what those plates are."

"Then tell us."

"They were part of the customs monitoring system."

"Monitoring what?"

"Pressure sensors. Door contacts. Movement."

"Audio?"

Tomas hesitated.

"Some rooms had microphones."

Lucia looked at him.

"Some?"

"This was before modern security."

Tomas showed them an old plan.

The archive.

Customs warehouse.

Service corridors.

Harbour.

Several lines connected them.

Not telephone lines.

Audio lines.

Elia traced one with his finger.

"Are they still connected?"

"Officially? No."

"Unofficially?"

Tomas looked at the wall.

"Old systems are rarely as dead as paperwork says."

They found a newer cable inside the maintenance hatch.

The insulation was less than a year old.

That was evidence.

Someone had maintained part of the old system.

But it did not yet prove why.

## Chapter 12 — The Wrong Timestamp

Elia tested an older recording.

Catalogue date: 2012.

Recorder model: discontinued in 2006.

Connector type: discontinued before 2010.

The evidence suggested the recording itself was older than the catalogue date.

But Elia refused to call it fraud.

There was another possibility.

A cataloguing error.

He searched the accession record.

The date matched the day the archive received the recording.

Not the day it was made.

Creation date and receipt date had been merged.

He showed Lucia.

"Could be an innocent archival mistake."

"Could."

"How do we distinguish it?"

"Find another one."

They checked Mara's record.

The same structure.

Then a third.

Same.

Three cases.

Same type of date confusion.

Lucia closed the file.

"Now it's a pattern."

Elia nodded.

Not proof of a conspiracy.

A pattern requiring an explanation.

## Chapter 13 — The Listener

The archive system registered a maintenance login at 02:14.

Elia was not there.

Lucia checked the camera.

Nobody entered.

The login had come through an old maintenance account.

They traced the route.

Hotel Aurora.

Then the service corridor.

Then the archive.

Elia looked at the map.

"Room Twelve."

Lucia shook her head.

"Maybe."

"Maybe?"

"We know the route passes through there. We don't know who used it."

They checked the modified files.

Mara.

Matteo.

Bassi.

Three records.

Nothing else.

The login disappeared.

Thirty-one seconds later, it returned.

Lucia took a photograph.

"We don't accuse anyone yet."

Elia nodded.

But when they left the archive, he looked back at the basement door.

For the first time, he had the uncomfortable feeling that the archive was not simply storing the investigation.

It was part of it.

## Chapter 14 — Lucia's Omission

Lucia brought the private copy to Elia.

"I should have given you this years ago."

"Why didn't you?"

"Because I couldn't authenticate it."

"That's not the whole reason."

"No."

She sat down.

"Your father asked me to leave Matteo's case alone."

Elia looked at her.

"Why?"

"He believed you were becoming obsessed."

"Was I?"

"Yes."

The answer hurt because she did not soften it.

Lucia continued.

"But I also believed the case had gaps."

She played the recording.

Matteo:

"Don't send this through the archive."

Unknown voice:

"Then where?"

"Room twelve."

The pulse followed.

Elia recognized the door sound.

"I heard that."

"When?"

"The night he disappeared."

Lucia looked at him.

"Why didn't you tell us?"

"Because I thought it was just a door."

Neither spoke.

That was the point.

A sound could be meaningless until another piece of evidence changed its meaning.

## Chapter 15 — The Second Recording

They finally listened to Mara's second file.

Elia did not listen alone.

Lucia sat beside him.

The file began with a ferry horn.

Three notes.

Then gulls.

A chain.

A gate.

A ventilation fan.

No speech.

Elia placed each sound on the town map.

The route formed slowly.

Harbour.

Freight line.

Customs corridor.

Hotel Aurora.

Tomas listened to the final section.

"That's the basement ventilation."

"Room Twelve?"

"Near it."

Elia replayed the end.

Three clicks.

The same three clicks from Mara's first recording.

Lucia said:

"She knew you would recognize the town."

"She knew I'd work with sound."

"Because you're an archivist."

Elia looked at the file.

"Or because she knew Matteo."

That possibility changed the emotional weight of the recording.

Mara had not chosen a random investigator.

She had chosen someone connected to the original disappearance.

## Chapter 16 — The Empty Room

The access lock to Room Twelve had been replaced.

Tomas opened it after several minutes.

Inside were old cables, a relay cabinet and a recorder connected to a newer power supply.

Elia photographed everything.

He did not touch the recorder.

A red indicator blinked.

Once.

Twice.

Then again after thirty-one seconds.

Tomas whispered:

"That shouldn't have power."

Lucia followed the cable.

It disappeared through the wall.

Behind a loose panel was a compartment.

Inside:

M.V.

E.V.

Elia stared at the initials.

"This could mean anything."

Lucia nodded.

"Exactly."

He looked at the recorder.

A voice came through the speaker.

Mara.

"If you've reached Room Twelve, they know you're looking."

The machine clicked.

Then stopped.

Elia listened to the silence.

"This isn't a storage room."

"What is it?"

He looked at the cable disappearing into the wall.

"A relay."

Lucia did not answer.

Neither did Tomas.

For the first time, the three of them understood that the recordings were not appearing by accident.

Something was moving them.

And whatever controlled that system had just learned that Elia had found it.
