# B11 LINE EDIT — CHAPTERS 1–8

## Chapter 1 — 08:17

The dead woman's voice arrived on a Tuesday morning.

Elia Valli was cleaning hiss from a recording of a town council meeting when an envelope appeared beneath the glass archive door.

It was 08:17.

The archive opened at nine.

Rain had turned the street to grey glass. Elia was alone except for the heating pipes, which knocked inside the walls whenever the weather changed.

Three knocks came from the wall behind him.

He stopped the recording.

Two more.

Old building, he thought.

Then he went to the door.

The envelope had his name on it.

**ELIA VALLI**

Nothing else.

Inside was a cassette.

**MARA VENTURI — 23:40 — 14 OCTOBER 2014**

He knew the name.

Mara Venturi had died that morning.

Elia checked the municipal death record before touching the cassette.

11:12.

He checked the hospital notification.

11:12.

He checked the police intake.

11:12.

The cassette said 23:40.

He called no one.

Not because he trusted the tape.

Because he wanted to know what kind of evidence it was before someone else told him what it meant.

He digitized the first thirty seconds.

Hiss.

A chair.

A kettle.

A gull.

Then Mara's voice.

"Is it recording?"

A man answered from farther away.

"I think so."

Mara laughed quietly.

Then:

"You'll have to tell him."

"Tell who?"

A pause.

"Elia."

He stopped breathing.

She came closer to the microphone.

"Elia, if you are hearing this, don't let them play the last minute."

The tape cut.

Not faded.

Cut.

Elia looked at the counter.

00:00.

Behind him, from the corridor:

"Don't."

He turned.

Nobody.

This time he called Lucia Serra.

## Chapter 2 — The Catalogue

Lucia arrived before nine.

She did not touch the cassette.

"Where did you get it?"

"Under the door."

"Who knew you would be here?"

"Nobody."

"That's not what I asked."

Elia looked at her.

"Nobody should have."

She photographed the envelope.

Then the label.

Then the evidence bag.

Only after that did Elia play the recording.

When Mara said his name, Lucia's expression changed.

"You never met her?"

"No."

"Your brother did."

Elia looked at her.

"How much did you know about Matteo?"

"Not enough."

That answer irritated him.

Lucia noticed.

"Don't turn this into Matteo yet."

"Then what is it?"

"Evidence of unknown provenance."

She pointed to the cassette.

"The date may be false."

"The death record may be false."

"Yes."

"But you don't believe either."

"I don't believe either one yet."

Elia liked that answer.

It was the first thing that made him trust her.

## Chapter 3 — Room Tone

They worked separately.

Lucia checked the death investigation.

Elia examined the sound.

The recording contained a low mechanical pulse every thirty-one seconds.

He isolated it.

Then found a second layer beneath the room tone.

A woman's voice whispered:

"Room twelve."

Lucia returned.

Elia played it.

"Could be a room."

"There were eleven rooms in the old Venturi house."

"Then it isn't."

He showed her the old property plan.

Beneath the house was a former customs service corridor.

Number twelve existed there.

Lucia stared at it.

"That's not in the current plans."

"That's why I think it matters."

His phone vibrated.

Unknown number.

An audio file named **MARA VENTURI 02**.

Lucia said, "Don't open it here."

"Why?"

"Because if someone wants you to listen, they may also want you to do it somewhere they control."

That was the first time Elia understood that the recording might be a trap.

## Chapter 4 — Official Time

Lucia confirmed the death time.

11:12.

But she also found a discrepancy in the original case file.

The first emergency call was logged at 10:58.

The medical examiner arrived at 11:34.

The difference meant nothing by itself.

But the background of Mara's recording contained a ferry horn.

Via della Marina, 6 had not been acoustically exposed to the harbour in the way the recording suggested.

Elia wrote that down.

Lucia stopped him.

"Don't write conclusions."

"It's a clue."

"Then write what you heard."

He changed it:

**Three-note ferry horn in recording. Source not yet established.**

Lucia nodded.

"That's how we keep evidence from becoming a story."

The sentence stayed with him.

## Chapter 5 — Room Twelve

Tomas Rinaldi showed them the old plans.

He knew more about the basement than he had first admitted.

"Why didn't you tell us about Room 12?"

"Because nobody asks about things that were filled with concrete."

"Was it filled?"

Tomas hesitated.

"Partly."

The access door had a new lock.

The dust around it had been disturbed.

But there was no proof that someone had entered recently.

Elia noticed a faint scrape on the lock plate.

Tomas said:

"That could be from the renovation."

Lucia agreed.

Elia did not.

But he wrote both possibilities in his notebook.

For the first time, he understood the difference between suspicion and evidence.

## Chapter 6 — Sofia's Silence

Sofia gave them Mara's message only after Lucia left the café.

"She told me not to give this to police."

"Why?"

"Because she thought someone inside the system could see it."

Elia read the message.

**If they ask about the old listening rooms, say you don't know what I'm talking about.**

"Did Mara mention Vieri?"

"No."

"Did she mention Matteo?"

Sofia looked away.

"She said your brother had made a mistake."

"What mistake?"

"I don't know."

That was not entirely true.

Elia could see it.

But Sofia did not yield.

Instead she handed him Mara's handwritten note.

**If Elia comes, give him the map.**

The note contained no map.

Only a list of sounds.

Ferry.

Gate.

Fan.

Relay.

Sofia said:

"I was afraid that if I gave you everything, I would be helping the person who wanted you to follow it."

## Chapter 7 — Matteo

Lucia finally played the old fragment.

Matteo's voice filled the room.

"Don't send this through the archive."

Another voice:

"Then where?"

"Room twelve."

The recording ended.

Elia did not speak.

Lucia waited.

After a while he said:

"I destroyed something like this."

"What?"

"An audio file."

"When?"

"After Matteo disappeared."

"Why?"

Elia looked at the floor.

"Because my father said I was turning grief into an investigation."

Lucia said nothing.

That silence was better than sympathy.

Elia continued:

"I thought I had destroyed the only copy."

Lucia looked at the recorder.

"Maybe you didn't."

## Chapter 8 — The First False Theory

The first theory was simple.

Mara had survived.

The impossible timestamp was real.

The recording had been made at 23:40.

Someone had helped her disappear.

Elia followed the theory until the evidence broke it.

A municipal access record showed Mara's credentials at 23:51.

But the raw server log showed the event had been created in 2019.

The apparent post-death activity was not contemporary evidence.

It was a later insertion.

Elia deleted the theory from his notebook.

Then his phone vibrated.

Unknown number.

**Good.**

A second message.

**Now you know the first lie.**

Lucia read it.

"Whoever sent this knew what you were testing."

Elia looked at the cassette.

"No."

He thought about the missing minute.

"They knew what I would test."

Neither of them spoke.

The investigation had changed.

They were no longer only examining Mara's recording.

Someone was anticipating their investigation.
