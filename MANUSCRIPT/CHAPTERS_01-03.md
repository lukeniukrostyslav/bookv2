# MANUSCRIPT — OPENING

## Chapter 1 — 08:17

The dead woman's voice arrived on a Tuesday morning.

Elia Valli was halfway through cleaning the hiss from a recording of a town council meeting when the envelope appeared beneath the glass door of the archive.

He noticed it because nobody used the glass door.

The archive opened at nine.

It was 08:17.

Rain had been falling since before dawn, hard enough to turn the street outside into a blurred strip of grey and yellow. The municipal building was empty except for Elia, two floors of paper, and the old heating pipes that knocked whenever the weather changed.

He stopped the audio.

The room became silent.

Then came three knocks from inside the wall.

Elia looked toward the corridor.

Nothing.

He waited.

Two more knocks.

He smiled without meaning to. Old buildings made old noises.

He went to the door.

The envelope was cream-coloured, unmarked except for his name.

ELIA VALLI.

Not Archivio Comunale. Not Signor Valli.

Just his name.

He opened the door and looked both ways.

Nobody.

The street was empty.

He picked up the envelope.

There was no stamp.

Inside was a cassette.

Not a USB drive. Not a disc.

A cassette.

The plastic case was scratched and clouded with age. A strip of white tape had been fixed to the spine. On it, in black ink, someone had written:

MARA VENTURI  
23:40  
14 OCTOBER 2014

Elia stared at the date.

He knew the name.

Everyone in Porto Nero knew the name.

Mara Venturi had died ten years ago.

He carried the cassette into the restoration room.

The archive owned three cassette decks. Only one still worked.

Elia put the tape in.

He almost pressed play.

Then stopped.

He checked the label again.

Mara Venturi.

He searched the municipal catalogue.

The result appeared immediately.

VENTURI, MARA  
Born: 1981  
Died: 14 October 2014  
Time of death: 11:12  
Location: Via della Marina, 6  
Cause: acute cardiac event.

Elia read the line twice.

Then he looked at the cassette.

23:40.

The same date.

Twelve hours and twenty-eight minutes after the recorded time of death.

He should have called someone.

Instead, he pressed PLAY.

For six seconds there was only hiss.

Then a woman's breathing.

Slow.

Close to the microphone.

A chair scraped across a floor.

A kettle clicked.

Somewhere outside, a gull screamed.

Elia leaned closer.

A woman's voice said:

"Is it recording?"

A man's voice answered from farther away.

"I think so."

Elia froze.

The man's voice was too faint to identify.

The woman laughed.

Not happily.

Then she said:

"You'll have to tell him."

"Tell who?"

There was a pause.

"Elia."

The tape clicked.

The room seemed to contract around him.

The woman's breathing changed.

She came closer to the microphone.

"Elia, if you are hearing this, don't let them play the last minute."

A sharp noise followed.

The tape continued.

Elia looked at the counter.

04:03.

03:58.

03:57.

He had not noticed that the machine had already reached the final minute.

His hand moved toward STOP.

The tape went silent.

Not the soft silence of an empty recording.

A cut.

Clean.

Absolute.

The counter reached 00:00.

Elia pressed REWIND.

The machine screamed.

He pulled his hand away.

Behind him, from the corridor, somebody whispered:

"Don't."

Elia turned.

Nobody was there.

## Chapter 2 — The Catalogue

He searched the building twice.

First quickly.

Then properly.

There was nobody in the corridor.

The side entrance was locked from the inside. The stairwell was empty. The basement door had not moved; its dust seal was unbroken.

Elia returned to the restoration room and closed the door.

He did not play the tape again.

Instead he opened the archive's death records.

Mara Venturi.

There it was.

11:12.

He found the scanned death certificate.

11:12.

He found the hospital notification.

11:12.

He found the newspaper clipping.

"MARA VENTURI DIES SUDDENLY AT 33."

11:12.

Everything agreed.

Except the cassette.

He opened the catalogue for audio donations.

Nothing.

There was no record of the tape.

He checked accession numbers.

Nothing.

He checked the physical storage register.

Nothing.

Then he noticed something that made him stop.

The handwriting on the cassette label was not old.

The ink was black and clean.

Elia knew handwriting.

For twenty years he had spent his life identifying what machines, people and time did to recorded evidence.

The label had been written recently.

Very recently.

He picked up his phone.

He had one person in town he could call.

Inspector Lucia Serra.

He knew her from the archive's work with the police. She was careful, impatient, and disliked coincidences.

Elia typed:

I have something you need to see.

He stared at the message.

Then deleted it.

He did not yet know why.

Maybe because the moment he involved the police, the tape would stop being his discovery and become evidence.

Maybe because he was afraid they would take it away.

Or maybe because the voice had said his name.

He put the cassette into an evidence bag.

On the back of the bag he wrote:

RECEIVED 08:17.

Then, beneath it:

SOURCE UNKNOWN.

He was about to place it in the locked cabinet when his phone vibrated.

Unknown number.

A message.

**You heard the voice.**

Elia did not move.

Another message arrived.

**Now listen to what she didn't say.**

## Chapter 3 — Room Tone

At 09:41, Elia learned that silence could have a fingerprint.

He returned to the tape.

He digitized it at high resolution and opened the waveform on his workstation.

The spoken words occupied less than four minutes.

The rest was room tone: refrigerator hum, distant traffic, rain, gulls, a faint metallic vibration.

Ordinary sounds.

Except they were not ordinary.

Elia isolated the background layer.

The refrigerator was cycling at a regular interval.

The gull appeared twice.

The metallic vibration appeared every thirty-one seconds.

He wrote the intervals down.

31.

31.

31.

31.

Then he increased the gain.

Under the room tone was another sound.

A low pulse.

Not speech.

Not music.

A machine.

Elia listened again.

The pulse stopped whenever Mara spoke.

Started again when she stopped.

He checked the spectrum.

There.

A second recording was buried beneath the first.

Not audible in the original playback.

A recording inside the recording.

Elia stared at the screen.

He separated the frequencies.

The hidden track became clearer.

Someone was reading numbers.

"...fourteen..."

Static.

"...seven..."

Static.

"...twenty-two..."

Then a woman's voice.

Not Mara.

Another woman.

She whispered:

"Room twelve."

Elia stopped the playback.

His first thought was the hospital.

Mara had died in a hospital, according to the newspaper.

But the certificate said Via della Marina.

He opened the municipal property map.

Via della Marina, 6.

The address where Mara had died.

The building had been demolished in 2019.

In its place stood a hotel.

Elia searched old plans.

The original building had eleven rooms.

He stared at the screen.

Then he remembered the hidden voice.

Room twelve.

His phone vibrated again.

Unknown number.

This time there was no text.

There was an audio attachment.

Duration:

**00:01:03.**

Elia looked at the filename.

MARA_VENTURI_02.

He did not press play.

Not yet.
