# MANUSCRIPT — CONTINUATION

## Chapter 4 — Official Time

At 10:06, Elia called Lucia Serra.

She answered on the fourth ring.

"What?"

"I need you to verify a death."

A pause.

"That's an unusual way to start a Tuesday."

"Mara Venturi."

The pause became longer.

"Where are you?"

"Archive."

"Stay there."

Lucia arrived twelve minutes later wearing a dark raincoat still wet at the shoulders. She did not take off her coat.

Elia put the cassette on the table.

Lucia read the label.

"Where did you get this?"

"Under the door."

"Who delivered it?"

"I don't know."

"Did you touch the envelope?"

"Yes."

"Good."

She looked at him.

"Why is that good?"

"Because if someone wanted you to think this was evidence, they succeeded."

Elia played the first section.

Lucia listened without moving.

When Mara said his name, Lucia looked at him.

When the tape stopped before the last minute, she said nothing.

Then Elia played the hidden layer.

"Room twelve."

Lucia wrote it down.

"Your source?"

"Anonymous."

"How anonymous?"

"Unknown number."

"Show me."

Elia showed her the messages.

Lucia photographed the screen.

"Now tell me what you know about Mara."

"She died at 11:12."

"According to?"

"Hospital record, death certificate, newspaper."

"Not enough."

Elia stared at her.

Lucia sighed.

"Come with me."

At the police station, she opened the original case file.

Mara Venturi had been found at Via della Marina, 6.

11:12 was the recorded time of death.

But the evidence log contained another time.

10:58.

Lucia pointed to it.

"Medical examiner arrived at 11:34. The first call came at 10:58."

Elia frowned.

"So?"

"Nothing."

"Lucia."

She closed the file.

"That's the point. Nothing."

On the way back to the archive, Elia kept thinking about the recording.

Then he remembered the kettle.

A kettle clicking off.

Mara had supposedly died in the house.

But the background contained a sound that did not belong there.

He reopened the waveform.

Under the kettle was a long metallic vibration.

Then, almost beneath it, a ferry horn.

Three low notes.

Elia knew that sound.

It came from the harbour.

Not from Via della Marina, 6.

At least not anymore.

## Chapter 5 — Room Twelve

The Hotel Aurora had replaced the Venturi house so completely that Elia could not see the old building when he stood outside.

Glass.

Stone.

Polished brass.

Tourists drinking coffee beneath heaters.

Tomas Rinaldi met him beside the service entrance.

"Lucia said you needed the plans."

"I need the old plans."

Tomas looked at him.

"That's usually more expensive."

They went downstairs.

The hotel smelled of detergent, damp concrete and coffee grounds.

Tomas unlocked a steel cabinet.

"The original property drawings."

He spread them across a workbench.

Elia found the old Venturi building.

Eleven rooms.

Then he found a second plan.

Customs infrastructure.

Service corridors.

Storage rooms.

Numbered spaces.

Room 12.

"Here."

Tomas leaned over.

"That shouldn't exist."

"It does."

"It existed."

He traced the corridor.

"This section was connected to the customs warehouse."

"And now?"

"Most of it was filled."

"Most?"

Tomas smiled without humour.

"Old buildings are good at keeping secrets."

They followed the plan.

A concrete wall stood behind a modern electrical cabinet.

Tomas removed the panel.

Behind it was a narrow metal door.

A faded number remained above the lock.

12.

Elia touched the handle.

The dust around it had been disturbed.

Not recently enough to look obvious.

Recently enough for an archivist to notice.

"Someone's been here."

Tomas crouched.

"Yes."

"How long ago?"

"Days."

Elia looked at him.

"Can you open it?"

"No."

"Why?"

"Because somebody replaced the lock."

## Chapter 6 — Sofia's Silence

Sofia Venturi agreed to meet at a café near the harbour.

She was younger than Elia expected.

Mara had always looked older in photographs.

More tired.

Sofia carried an envelope.

"I should have given this to the police."

"Why didn't you?"

"Because Mara told me not to."

Elia waited.

Sofia opened the envelope.

Inside was a printed message.

Three days before Mara died.

**If they ask about the old listening rooms, say you don't know what I'm talking about.**

Elia read it twice.

"Who are they?"

"I don't know."

"Did Mara say?"

"No."

Sofia folded her hands.

"She was frightened."

"Of Vieri?"

Sofia looked up sharply.

"You know Alessandro?"

"I know his name."

"Everyone knows his name."

She looked toward the window.

"Mara hated people who turned history into property."

"That's not an answer."

"No."

Sofia pushed another sheet toward him.

A handwritten note.

Mara's handwriting.

**If Elia comes, give him the map.**

Elia felt something cold move through his chest.

"She knew me?"

"She knew your brother."

The café seemed suddenly louder.

"Matteo?"

Sofia nodded.

"She met him several times."

"Why?"

"I don't know."

"You must know something."

"I know she asked him to leave it alone."

"What?"

"The listening rooms."

Elia looked at the note again.

At the bottom was a line he had not noticed.

**The sound always arrives before the story.**

## Chapter 7 — Matteo

Lucia did not want to reopen the file.

Elia understood why.

Old disappearances were not doors.

They were walls.

People had built lives around them.

"You said there was an audio device."

Lucia looked out of her office window.

"Who told you?"

"You."

"I said there was a fragment."

"You said it was not in the public file."

"It wasn't."

"Why?"

Lucia rubbed her forehead.

"Because we couldn't authenticate it."

"Play it."

"No."

"Why?"

"Because you are not objective."

Elia laughed.

"Neither are you."

That made her look at him.

Finally she opened a drawer.

Inside was a small digital recorder.

"I kept a copy."

The file was only twenty-three seconds.

A man's voice.

Matteo.

Elia recognized him immediately.

Older than memory.

Angrier.

"Don't send this through the archive."

A second voice answered.

Elia did not recognize it.

"Then where?"

Matteo said:

"Room twelve."

The recording ended.

Elia stared at the screen.

There was the 31-second pulse.

But beneath it was something else.

Water.

A ferry engine.

And a metal gate closing.

He had heard that gate eleven years ago.

He had waited outside the customs warehouse while Matteo argued with someone inside.

At the time, Elia had believed it was an ordinary argument.

Now he wasn't sure.

## Chapter 8 — The First False Theory

By evening, Elia had built a theory.

Mara had not died when everyone thought.

Someone had helped her disappear.

The timestamp was real.

The recording was real.

Room twelve was real.

Maybe Mara had staged the death.

Maybe the official cause of death was false.

The theory explained too much.

That was its first problem.

Its second problem was the access record.

At 23:51 on the night of Mara's death, a municipal system recorded an access event under her credentials.

Elia stared at it.

Then he checked the raw system log.

The event had been created six years later.

The catalogue showed the event as historical.

The raw log showed its creation date.

2019.

Someone had manufactured the history.

Elia sat back.

The theory collapsed.

Mara had not been alive at 23:51.

Someone wanted him to believe she had been.

His phone vibrated.

Unknown number.

One message.

**Good. Now you know the first lie.**

Elia looked toward the archive door.

For the first time, he understood that the recording had never been an invitation.

It was a test.

And somebody was grading him.
