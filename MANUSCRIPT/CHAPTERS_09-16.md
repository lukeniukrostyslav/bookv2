# MANUSCRIPT — CHAPTERS 09-16

## Chapter 9 — Three Voices

Elia spent the next morning listening to people who were no longer there.

The first recording belonged to a fisherman named Carlo Bassi, missing since 2008.

The second belonged to a customs electrician who disappeared in 2011.

Neither case had ever been connected to Mara.

But both recordings contained the same pulse.

Thirty-one seconds.

Elia marked the waveform.

The pulse was followed by a tiny metallic click.

He played Mara's tape.

Click.

Bassi.

Click.

The electrician.

Click.

Three rooms.

Three years.

One sound.

Lucia stood behind him.

"Could be the same equipment."

"That's what I'm trying to prove."

"And if it is?"

Elia looked at the screen.

"Then Mara wasn't investigating a recording."

"What was she investigating?"

"Who had access to the room that made it."

## Chapter 10 — 31 Seconds

They moved the recordings into separate channels.

Elia removed speech.

Then rain.

Then engines.

The pulse remained.

It was not an acoustic echo.

It was a mechanical cycle.

Thirty-one seconds exactly.

Lucia watched him work.

"Could someone fake it?"

"Yes."

"Could three different people fake it independently?"

"Yes."

"But not by accident."

"No."

Elia enlarged the spectrum.

A narrow band appeared at the edge of the pulse.

A motor.

Low voltage.

Old equipment.

He remembered the door beneath Hotel Aurora.

Room 12.

The next sound was almost inaudible.

A relay switching.

Elia wrote one sentence in his notebook:

**The machine is older than the recordings.**

Then he crossed out "machine."

He wrote:

**The system is older than the recordings.**

## Chapter 11 — The Old System

Tomas took them to the basement after closing.

He carried a flashlight even though the lights worked.

"Why the flashlight?"

"Because old wiring lies."

The service corridor ended at a sealed concrete section.

Tomas pointed to a row of metal plates.

"These were part of the customs monitoring system."

"For what?"

"Pressure. Doors. Movement."

"Audio?"

Tomas hesitated.

"Not officially."

Elia touched one of the plates.

The metal was cold.

Behind it came a faint vibration.

Thirty-one seconds.

He looked at Tomas.

"You hear that?"

Tomas nodded.

"I've heard it for years."

"Why didn't you report it?"

"To whom?"

That answer stayed with Elia.

They found a maintenance hatch.

Inside was a cable bundle leading toward the harbour.

One cable was new.

The insulation was less than a year old.

Officially, the system had been decommissioned twenty-seven years earlier.

Someone had kept it alive.

## Chapter 12 — The Wrong Timestamp

Elia returned to the archive alone.

He selected the Bassi recording.

The catalogue said:

Recorded: 2012.

The tape itself said something else.

The formulation of the magnetic layer was consistent with media manufactured before 2010.

That alone proved little.

Then he found the recorder model used for the capture.

It had been discontinued in 2006.

He checked the original case.

The microphone cable had a connector that had not been manufactured after 2009.

The recording could not have been made in 2012.

The catalogue date was false.

Elia leaned back.

Then he noticed something worse.

The date was not random.

It corresponded to the date the recording entered the municipal archive.

Someone had confused—or deliberately replaced—**creation time with receipt time**.

He opened Mara's record.

The same structure appeared.

He whispered:

"They aren't changing the recording."

He looked at the clock.

"They're changing when we believe it was recorded."

## Chapter 13 — The Listener

At 02:14, the archive system registered a login.

Elia was not there.

Nobody was supposed to be there.

The access came through an old maintenance account.

He called Lucia.

She arrived before dawn.

They checked the camera.

No one entered.

"Then how did they log in?"

Elia showed her the network map.

"The maintenance line."

"The one under the hotel?"

"Part of it."

Lucia stared at the screen.

"Someone can reach the archive without walking into the archive."

"Yes."

They checked the modified files.

Only three had been opened.

Mara.

Matteo.

And one older disappearance.

The system closed the session.

Thirty-one seconds later, the same login appeared again.

Then disappeared.

Lucia said:

"They're listening."

Elia corrected her.

"No."

He looked toward the floor.

"They're waiting."

## Chapter 14 — Lucia's Omission

Lucia finally gave him the private copy.

She had kept it for eleven years.

"I thought it might matter someday."

"Why didn't you tell me?"

"Because your father asked me not to."

Elia stared at her.

"My father knew?"

"Not everything."

"Enough."

Lucia did not answer.

The recording played again.

Matteo:

"Don't send this through the archive."

Unknown voice:

"Then where?"

Matteo:

"Room twelve."

The 31-second pulse.

Then another sound.

A door.

Elia closed his eyes.

He remembered that door.

He had heard it on the night Matteo disappeared.

Lucia said quietly:

"You were there."

"Outside."

"You never told us."

"I didn't know what was happening."

"Neither did I."

Elia looked at her.

"Then why keep this?"

"Because I knew the official version wasn't complete."

## Chapter 15 — The Second Recording

The second file was not a message.

It was a map made from sound.

A ferry horn.

Three gulls.

A chain dragged across concrete.

A mechanical gate.

A bell from the harbour.

Elia placed each sound on a map.

Tomas watched.

"You can't locate a sound like that."

"I can if the town hasn't changed."

By noon they had a route.

Harbour.

Freight line.

Old customs corridor.

Hotel Aurora.

The final sound was a ventilation fan.

Tomas recognized it.

"Basement."

"Room twelve?"

Tomas nodded.

Elia opened the original file again.

At the end of the recording, just before the hidden voice:

three clicks.

The same three clicks appeared in the second file.

Mara had not sent directions.

She had sent an acoustic route.

## Chapter 16 — The Empty Room

The lock on Room 12 had been replaced.

Tomas brought tools.

Inside, the room was smaller than Elia expected.

No furniture.

No windows.

Only old cables.

And a recorder.

Not museum equipment.

Something newer.

Connected.

Powered.

Working.

Elia did not touch it.

A red light blinked.

Once.

Twice.

Then every thirty-one seconds.

He followed the cable to the wall.

Behind the panel was a small compartment.

Inside lay a strip of paper.

Three letters.

**M.V.**

Below them:

**E.V.**

Elia stopped breathing.

"Matteo," he said.

Tomas did not answer.

A sound came from the recorder.

A woman's voice.

Mara.

Not a recording they had heard before.

"If you've reached Room Twelve, they already know."

The machine clicked.

Then went silent.

Elia looked at Lucia.

"We were wrong."

She understood.

"What?"

"This isn't where the recordings are stored."

He looked at the cables disappearing into the wall.

"It's where they're sent."
