# B3 — STORY PROMISE / HOOK

## Canonical hook

At 08:17 on a rainy Tuesday morning, sound archivist **Elia Valli** receives a damaged cassette from the municipal archive of the coastal town of **Porto Nero**.

The label says:

**MARA VENTURI — 23:40 — 14 OCTOBER 2014**

Mara Venturi died at 11:12 that morning.

The tape should not exist.

When Elia restores the recording, he hears Mara breathing, moving around a kitchen, and speaking to someone who is not recorded on the tape. Then she says one sentence:

**“Elia, if you are hearing this, don't let them play the last minute.”**

Elia has never met Mara.

The final minute is missing.

## Story promise

The reader will follow Elia as he tries to answer four escalating questions:

1. How was the recording made after Mara's death?
2. Why was it sent to Elia?
3. Who else has received impossible recordings?
4. What is contained in the missing final minute?

The final explanation must be coherent, seeded through fair clues, and emotionally connected to Elia rather than being a random mechanical trick.

## Opening principles

- Begin with a concrete anomaly, not exposition.
- Establish Elia's profession through action.
- Make the recording itself a character-like presence.
- Give the reader one answer and at least two better questions.
- End early chapters with meaningful reversals rather than artificial cliffhangers.
- Keep supernatural interpretation possible but unconfirmed.

## Commercial positioning

The concept combines a high-concept mystery with psychological tension and a seaside setting.
